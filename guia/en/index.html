In brief

Robô caixa fixa is a 3D-printed mount project for anyone who wants to build a desktop robot with less mechanical work. It uses the Waveshare ESP32-S3 module with a 1.54-inch screen, keeping its original casing and electronics. Download two parts, test the cradle first, then print the base with raised INEMA lettering in the same color. You will need a 3D printer, Bambu Studio, filament, the module, and screws. The design was checked on a computer; the physical fit and AI conversation have not been validated in this project.

One fixed head. The whole module.

The screen, audio, and optional battery remain part of the original device. The mount holds the assembly and leaves the front clear for touch.

What you assemble

Base with a fixed column and removable cradle (the piece that holds the screen module). Two M3 × 16 screws and two M3 nuts, still to be checked against the actual parts.

The mark on the base

INEMA raised by 1.2 mm, with thickened letters shifted 4 mm inward from the base. One solid piece, in the same filament color.

What comes later

Face animation program and AI conversation. A local computer and a direct connection to an API are future options, not included features.

This is a prototype. Nothing has been printed or assembled for this project. The module drops in from above and is held by gravity: do not lift the assembly by holding the module. Audio, cable access, and stability while touching the screen need physical testing.

Which module to use

The selected listing is the ESP32-S3 with a 1.54″ screen. The photos show versions with and without a battery, and with and without touch. All are listed with a 1.54″ screen; the larger black front on the touch version does not mean a larger display area.

The official drawing lists 46 × 46 × 22.5 mm for the casing shown. Measure your version before printing. Do not confuse it with the M5Stack CoreS3 Lite or SpotPear Muma: their mounts and boards are different.

Technical section

From file to fit test

You do not need this section to understand the design above. To print, follow the steps below, starting with the cradle.

Download the cradle first

The ZIP includes both STL files, the 3D assembly, the geometry report, and the instructions (in Portuguese). It does not include G-code or a validated 3MF profile. The base measures 76 × 64 mm; assembled height is 78.4 mm.

Slice in Bambu Studio

Slicing turns a model into layers and movements for the printer. Do this on your computer, not on this page.

1

Choose your printer

Open Bambu Studio. Select Bambu Lab A1, a 0.4 mm nozzle, the installed build plate, and your actual filament. If you need to install it, use the official download.

2

Open the cradle only

Download the first STL above and drag the file onto the virtual plate. Keep the scale at 100%. The cradle was exported resting on its back; do not rotate it to match how it looks when assembled in the photo.

Expected size on the plate: 51.6 × 24 × 31.9 mm. A very different dimension indicates an incorrect scale or orientation.

3

Set up the test

  • Initial proposal, not yet tested: PETG, 0.20 mm layer height, four walls, and 25% infill.
  • In the Filament panel, select your PETG brand or Generic PETG.
  • Under Process, choose a 0.20 mm profile.
  • In the Quality tab, check Layer height: 0.20 mm.
  • In the Strength tab, set Wall loops: 4 and Sparse infill density: 25%.
  • The walls form the part’s outer shell; infill is the structure inside it.
  • If a field is hidden, enable Advanced mode.
  • Do not select PLA when printing with PETG.
  • In the Support tab, for this first test, select Enable support and the Normal auto type, leaving the other profile values unchanged.
  • Support is a temporary structure that holds up sections that would otherwise have no plastic underneath.
  • It can use extra material and may need to be removed afterward.
  • This setting has not yet been checked in this slicer: if the preview shows a warning or an unsupported area, do not start printing until you resolve it.
4

Click Slice Plate

The button may also appear as Fatiar mesa. In the Preview tab, scroll through the layers. Check that the first layer rests on the plate, no part starts in midair, and required supports are present. The software will report the actual time and filament usage.

If you see a mesh or orientation warning, stop before printing and check the file. The STL files passed geometry checks, but that does not replace reviewing the slicer preview.

5

Print and test the cradle

  • One option is to use the A1’s microSD card: after slicing, open File → Export → Export plate sliced file (it may also appear in the arrow menu next to Print plate).
  • Save the sliced file to your computer, copy it to the card, and safely eject the card.
  • Use the file with print instructions, usually ending in .gcode.3mf—not the STL or a project 3MF.
  • Insert the card into the A1, open the card’s file list on the screen, select the part, and check the plate and filament before starting.
  • If the file does not appear, return to Bambu Studio and confirm that you exported the sliced file.
  • After printing, let the part cool and remove the temporary support.
  • With the module turned off, test inserting it from above without forcing the screen or case.
  • Check the buttons, connectors, and audio.
  • If it feels tight, stop and do not print the base.
  • Measure the outside width and thickness of your module case with calipers, then ask the project lead to adjust the cradle.
  • Do not force the fit or scale up the entire model: that also changes the screw spacing.
6

Only then print the base

Open the second STL, keep the base resting on the build plate, and repeat the slicing process. INEMA is already part of the piece, so use a single color. Join the base and cradle with two M3 × 16 screws and two M3 nuts before inserting the module. Check screw length, USB clearance, and stability when touching the screen.

What was checked

  • Both meshes are closed, have positive volume, and contain one body per file.
  • The parts fit on the A1's 256 mm build plate.
  • The parts do not occupy the same space as the rectangular box used to represent the module in the test.
  • Removal from above was checked on a computer in five positions.
  • The negative test detects interference when the simulated module is widened.

Still to be done: review the sliced files, physical printing, fit with your variant, actual USB plug, acoustics, and stability. No measured print time is available.
